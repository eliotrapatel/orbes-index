# ORBES CODE-01 — scan test matrix

Generated by `npx tsx scripts/scan-matrix.ts` (camera simulator `test/support/camera-sim.ts`, decoder `src/core/decoder/`).
Every condition runs 20 seeded trials, each with a real CODE-01 artifact (seeded payload, CRC-valid frame, encoder output) photographed in a 1280×720 frame and decoded with default options. A trial succeeds only when the decoded 79 data bytes equal the encoded ones. Times are decoder wall-clock time per frame on the machine that generated this report (median / 95th percentile), single-threaded Node.js; failed frames are included, and they are the slow ones (every fallback stage runs before giving up).

Overall: median 34 ms, p95 259 ms per frame. False positives: **0 / 40** code-free frames (desk clutter scenes and uniform noise, mirrored reading enabled).

## Miscorrection safety

A miscorrected word that passes CRC-16 would reach the server as a well-formed code with a wrong signature and show a genuine piece as INVALID SIGNATURE (ORBES-CODE-SPEC §11). The decoder therefore never erases more than **70** codeword bytes, keeping at least 15 of the 85 parity bytes to check the rest (80 before 2026-10-01: 18 miscorrections in 100 000 random words at 80 erasures, 0 in 100 000 at 70), and the verify app holds a read whose correction load 2·errors + erasures exceeds 50 until a second, independent frame decodes to identical data.

In this run: largest erasure count of a correct read **50**; correct reads above the confirmation load **183 of 1779** (10.3 %): Module size (typicalPhone preset) · 2.25 px/u (20); Module size (lowLight preset) · 3.50 px/u (19); Defocus blur · σ 0.5 u (19); Specular glare · 15 % (15); Specular glare · 20 % (20); Occlusion · blob 10 % (1); Occlusion · blob 15 % (17); Occlusion · blob 20 % (20); Occlusion · strip 2 u (1); Cylindrical curvature · 0.5 rad (1); Cylindrical curvature · 0.7 rad (12); Cylindrical curvature · 0.9 rad (17); Camera simulator presets · leather (6); Camera simulator presets · worn (15). A held read costs one more frame (≥ 120 ms) in the live scanner. Lowering the cap from 80 to 70 left every success rate in this matrix unchanged (re-run of 2026-10-01 against the previous report).

## Smallest reliable printed size

Under the typicalPhone preset the decoder reaches ≥ 95 % success from **2.25 px per u** upward; under the pessimistic lowLight preset from **3.50 px per u**. The printed artifact is 50 u wide (quiet zone included). Converted to print sizes under stated assumptions about the phone:

| Assumed capture | Sampling | typicalPhone minimum (2.25 px/u) | lowLight minimum (3.50 px/u) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1080p stream, phone 12–15 cm away | ≈ 10 px/mm | **11.3 mm** | **17.5 mm** |
| 1080p stream, phone 8–10 cm away (close focus) | ≈ 16 px/mm | **7.0 mm** | **10.9 mm** |

Recommendation: do not print below **17.5 mm** (3.50 px/u at 10 px/mm: the larger of 1.5× the typicalPhone minimum and the lowLight minimum), and prefer 25 mm or more where the product allows.

Honest caveats: these figures come from a simulator, not from real phones. The simulator blurs with a fixed Gaussian and models no demosaicing, sharpening halos, denoising, rolling shutter or auto-exposure hunting, all of which cost real phones resolution; the minima above are therefore optimistic. They also assume the phone actually focuses at that distance (many main cameras cannot focus closer than 8–10 cm, so the close-up row is a best case), that the scanner receives the full-resolution stream rather than a downscaled preview, and print or engraving that holds the 0.28 u gaps between rings at that pitch. Real-device validation remains necessary before committing to a minimum size.
